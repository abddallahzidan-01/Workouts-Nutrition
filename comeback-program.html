Full gym · 5 days a week · returning after 3 years off

Rebuild.

Your muscles remember more than you think. This program gets your joints and tendons caught up first, then builds from there — without the injury that usually ends a comeback in week two.

Weeks 1–3 · Reactivation Week 4+ · Build

How this works

Two phases. The first rebuilds your foundation, the second builds size.

Three years off means your muscles still remember how to grow — that comes back fast. Your tendons, ligaments, and joints don't have the same memory, and they're what actually get hurt when someone returns to their old numbers too soon. So this program spends three weeks getting those tissues ready, then hands you a real 5-day hypertrophy split.

Soreness is normal. Expect more of it than you'd like in weeks 1–2 — that's detraining, not damage.

Sharp or joint pain is different. If something feels wrong in a joint rather than a muscle, drop the weight or swap the exercise.

Old injuries first. If anything was flaring up before your break, get it checked before loading it heavily again.

Before every session

5–10 minutes of light cardio plus dynamic movement for whatever you're training that day — leg swings and bodyweight squats before legs, band pull-aparts and arm circles before upper body. Your muscles are ready before your joints are; the warm-up is what closes that gap.

Search the exercise name plus "form" on YouTube before your first set — a 60-second video beats any written description for seeing exactly where to put your hands and feet. Ask in your chat with Claude too; images for any exercise on this page can be pulled up any time you want to see one.

Weeks 1–3 · 4 training days · Sun / Mon / Wed / Thu

Effort: RPE 6–7 — stop every set with 3–4 reps still left in you.

Tue / Fri / Sat: rest, a walk, or light mobility work. Nothing that leaves you sore.

Phase 2 — Build

Week 4 onward · 5 training days, each muscle hit twice a week

Progression: once every set of an exercise hits the top of its rep range with 1–2 reps left in the tank, add 2.5–5% load next session and drop back to the bottom of the range.

Deload every 6–8 weeks: cut volume by about 40–50% for a week, keep some intensity. This is what lets you keep progressing for months instead of stalling.

Nutrition

Muscle is built in the gym and paid for at the table.

Protein1.6–2.2 g/kg
Calorie surplus+10–15%
Fat0.6–1.0 g/kg
Meals/day3–4

Protein is the biggest lever you have — spread it across 3–4 meals rather than one big dose. Keep calories only modestly above maintenance; a bigger surplus just adds fat, not extra muscle. Don't cut carbs to make room for protein — they're what fuel the training that actually triggers growth.

Meal structure

Four meals a day covers this comfortably: three main meals plus one built around your workout. Aim for a palm-to-two-palms of protein at each meal — that habit gets you to your daily number more reliably than any specific meal timing.

Supplements worth considering

  • Creatine monohydrate — 3–5g daily, any time of day. The most well-researched supplement for strength and muscle gain; consistency matters more than timing.
  • Protein powder — whey or a plant blend, purely for convenience on days you're short on food-based protein.
  • Vitamin D — worth checking your levels, especially with limited sun exposure; supplement if you're deficient.
  • Caffeine — 100–200mg pre-workout can help performance; keep it earlier in the day if it affects your sleep.
  • Omega-3 / fish oil — general health support, not specific to muscle growth.

Supplements support the process — they don't replace training consistently and eating enough. Check with a doctor before adding anything new, especially if you're on medication.

Cooking oats

Warm, stovetop or microwave

80g oats + ~200ml milk or water. Microwave 2–3 min, stirring halfway, or simmer on the stove ~5 min until creamy. Top with banana and honey.

Overnight oats — best for your mornings

The night before: 80g oats + 200ml milk or yogurt + a spoon of honey in a jar, lid on, fridge overnight. No cooking, no morning effort — grab it straight after training.

You've got milk at work — use it. Carry a small bag of dry oats (80g) to your desk instead of prepping every night, and make it there with the office milk at your 10:30 AM snack. Save the overnight-oats method for the nights you don't feel like it.

Second option: bring a shaker and a scoop of protein powder, and mix it with the work milk as your 4 PM snack instead of yogurt. Either way, it's free protein and carbs you're not paying for or carrying from home.

When do you actually cook?

Your morning window is too tight for real cooking — this schedule moves it to when you actually have time.

  • Fri & Sat (your rest days): your main cooking session. Grill a big batch of chicken and/or fish, cook a large pot of rice, boil a dozen eggs. Portion into containers — that's most of your week's lunches and dinners done.
  • Every evening, 5 minutes: jar up tomorrow's overnight oats before bed.
  • Evenings after work: cook dinner fresh (you have time between 5 PM and Maghrib), and box up tomorrow's lunch from what's already prepped in the fridge.
  • Morning, after training: nothing to cook — just plate the eggs if you're doing them fresh, or grab the oats jar and go.
  • Keep frozen vegetables and pre-portioned snacks (nuts, yogurt cups) on hand for the days the batch-cooked food runs low.

Storing what you cook

Cooked rice needs more care than it looks like — bacteria that survive cooking can multiply if it sits out, and reheating doesn't fix that.

  • Cool it fast. Spread rice on a tray for 15–20 minutes rather than sealing it hot into the fridge.
  • Portion before you fridge it — single-meal containers cool faster and make grabbing lunch easier.
  • Fridge, 3–4 days max. If your Friday cooking session needs to last into day 5, freeze that portion instead of stretching the fridge time.
  • Freezer, up to a month. Thaw and reheat until steaming hot all the way through, and only reheat once.
  • Same 3–4 day fridge window applies to your batch-cooked chicken, fish, and meat.
